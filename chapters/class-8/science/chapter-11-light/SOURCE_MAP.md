## Tier 3 scientific verification

### S2 — OpenStax, Physics: Refraction

URL: `https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/16-2-refraction`

Supports:

- refraction as change in ray direction across material variations
- change in light speed/index as the cause of directional change for oblique incidence
- angle definitions relative to the perpendicular/normal
- critical-angle/TIR progression

Classroom use:

- clarify that entering a different medium does not guarantee visible deviation; at normal incidence, Snell's law gives zero refraction angle, so the ray continues undeviated while speed changes.

### S3 — OpenStax, University Physics Volume 3: Refraction

URL: `https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/1-3-refraction`

Supports:

- Snell-law scientific background
- larger speed/index change causing larger direction change at a given incidence angle
- ray/normal/angle terminology
- PhET Bending Light as a suitable interactive resource

### S4 — OpenStax, University Physics Volume 3: Total Internal Reflection

URL: `https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/1-4-total-internal-reflection`

Supports:

- critical angle: refracted angle = 90°
- TIR only when light travels toward a lower refractive-index medium
- TIR when incidence angle exceeds critical angle

### S5 — NCERT/Philoid, Ray Optics and Optical Instruments

URL: `https://philoid.com/ncert/chapter/leph201?y=2015`

Supports:

- for a rectangular/parallel-sided slab, the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray and suffers lateral displacement
- therefore equal incident/emergent-angle treatment is geometry-specific, not a universal arbitrary-interface rule

### S6 — NCERT Class XII Physics Laboratory Manual, glass-slab activity

URL: `https://www.ncert.nic.in/pdf/publication/sciencelaboratorymanuals/classXII/physics/lelm312.pdf`

Supports:

- measuring incidence and emergence angles for a glass slab
- checking that the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray

### S7 — OpenStax, Simple Magnifier

URL: `https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/2-7-the-simple-magnifier`

Supports:

- a convex lens used as a simple magnifier
- object within focal length gives a virtual, upright, enlarged image

### S8 — OpenStax, Image Formation by Lenses

URL: `https://openstax.org/books/college-physics/pages/25-6-image-formation-by-lenses`

Supports:

- real versus virtual image distinction
- convex lens can form real or virtual images depending on object position
- within focal length: virtual, upright, magnified image
- camera forms a real image

### S9 — OpenStax, Physics of the Eye

URL: `https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/26-1-physics-of-the-eye`

Supports:

- cornea + lens focus light onto retina
- retinal image must be real for clear vision
- eye changes optical power/focal length for different object distances
- accommodation concept
- pupil as variable opening controlling admitted light

### S10 — OpenStax, Vision Correction

URL: `https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-ap-courses/pages/26-2-vision-correction`

Supports:

- myopia corrected with a diverging spectacle lens
- hyperopia corrected with a converging spectacle lens
- corrective lens changes the incoming rays so the eye can focus the image on the retina

Classroom status:

- use only as clearly labelled supplementary repair/support for the NCTB spectacles outcome gap, not as evidence that the missing subsection exists in the textbook body.

### S11 — OpenStax, Dual Nature of Light

URL: `https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/21-3-the-dual-nature-of-light`

Supports:

- electromagnetic radiation exhibits wave-like and particle-like properties
- photon as a discrete quantum/particle-like description of electromagnetic radiation

Classroom limit:

- one short conceptual statement only; no quantum derivation or `E=hf` in CORE.

### S12 — NASA Science, Visible Light

URL: `https://science.nasa.gov/ems/09_visiblelight/`

Supports:

- visible light is the part of the electromagnetic spectrum detectable by the human eye
- visible range context if needed as FLEX/STRETCH

### S13 — Physics Classroom, Optical Density and Light Speed

URL: `https://www.physicsclassroom.com/class/refrn/Lesson-1/Optical-Density-and-Light-Speed`

Supports:

- optical density is not the same as physical/mass density
- greater optical density corresponds qualitatively to slower light propagation

Classroom treatment:

- keep the distinction qualitative; avoid turning 'optical density' into an unnecessary numerical subtopic.

### S14 — Kodak, Processing KODAK Motion Picture Films, Module 5

URL: `https://www.kodak.com/content/products-brochures/Film/Processing-KODAK-Motion-Picture-Films-Module-5.pdf`

Supports:

- thiosulfate/hypo fixer converts undeveloped insoluble silver halide into a soluble complex that can leave the emulsion
- film-camera chemical processing is distinct from digital imaging

### S15 — Chemistry LibreTexts, Precipitation Reactions in Photography

URL: `https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/General_Chemistry/Book%3A_General_Chemistry%3A_Principles_Patterns_and_Applications_%28Averill%29/04%3A_Reactions_in_Aqueous_Solution/4.05%3A_Precipitation_Reactions`

Supports:

- developer acts as a reducing agent, preferentially amplifying exposed silver-halide grains into metallic silver
- fixing removes unexposed silver halide with sodium thiosulfate ('hypo')
- the remaining metallic-silver pattern constitutes the negative in black-and-white film processing
