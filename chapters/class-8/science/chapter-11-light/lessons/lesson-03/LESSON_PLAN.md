## Learning goals

Students should be able to:

1. describe qualitatively how a convex lens converges light;
2. identify focus and focal length conceptually;
3. distinguish real/virtual, erect/inverted and magnified/reduced images at the level needed for this chapter;
4. explain how a magnifying glass forms a virtual, erect and enlarged image when the object is within the focal length;
5. identify major parts of the human eye and their functions;
6. explain qualitatively how iris/pupil regulate light and how accommodation changes focusing;
7. state the basic corrective role of spectacles, clearly labelled as supplementary because the NCTB body omits this subsection.

## Human-eye functions to emphasize

- cornea + lens: focus incoming light
- iris/pupil: regulate admitted light
- retina: light-sensitive receiving surface
- aqueous/vitreous humours: transparent media maintaining optical path/shape
- accommodation: eye changes optical power/focusing for different object distances

## Spectacles treatment

Use only a concise conceptual statement:

- spectacles place an additional lens before the eye to change incoming-ray convergence/divergence so the eye can focus a clear image on the retina;
- diverging lens for myopia and converging lens for hyperopia may be shown as a small verified supplementary visual;
- no lens-power calculation, detailed defect derivation or exam claim that this wording is from the NCTB body.

## Major misconceptions

- pupil is a solid black object rather than an opening;
- eye lens alone controls admitted light;
- convex lens always produces the same image type;
- magnifying glass always magnifies regardless of object position;
- spectacles make the eye physically larger/smaller rather than altering ray convergence.
