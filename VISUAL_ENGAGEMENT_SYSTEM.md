# Visual Engagement System

## Objective

Make every classroom deck visually attractive enough to capture and sustain student attention while preserving scientific accuracy, cognitive clarity, accessibility, and teaching pace.

Attractiveness is not decoration. Visual design must improve attention, comprehension, memory, curiosity, or participation.

## Design principles

### 1. One clear focal point per slide

Every slide must make the student's eye know where to look first. Use scale, spacing, contrast, position, and limited emphasis to establish hierarchy.

### 2. Visual storytelling over text dumping

Prefer labelled diagrams, before/after views, process steps, comparisons, timelines, worked examples, and progressive reveals over dense prose.

### 3. Coherence

Remove visual elements that do not support the teaching objective. Decorative imagery must be limited and must never compete with core information.

### 4. Signaling

Use headings, arrows, callouts, numbering, highlighting, and spatial grouping to show relationships and guide attention.

### 5. Segmentation

Complex processes should be broken into digestible stages rather than shown all at once when that would overload students.

### 6. Redundancy control

Do not place a long paragraph on-screen and simultaneously read the same paragraph aloud. Slides should support teacher explanation rather than duplicate it word-for-word.

## Attention rhythm

A 50-55 minute lesson should not remain visually or cognitively static for long stretches. Where pedagogically appropriate, include a meaningful attention reset roughly every 7-10 minutes.

Possible attention resets:

- prediction question
- quick poll or show-of-hands prompt
- reveal-before-answer slide
- visual comparison
- short worked step
- labelled diagram interaction
- mini-demo
- 30-90 second video/animation excerpt
- simulation observation
- pair explanation
- one-question retrieval check

This is a heuristic, not a rigid timer. Do not insert activity merely to satisfy cadence.

## Visual rhythm across a deck

Avoid 10 consecutive slides with the same layout. Rotate among appropriate slide families while maintaining a consistent design system.

Examples:

- strong visual hook
- concise concept slide
- diagram/process slide
- definition card
- example/application slide
- misconception challenge
- worked numerical
- prediction/practice slide
- simulation/video launch
- summary/exit slide

Variation should feel intentional, not chaotic.

## Grade calibration

### Class 8

- more concrete visuals and familiar real-life examples
- lower information density
- larger diagrams and shorter text blocks
- more guided questions and visual cues

### Class 9

- balanced visuals, definitions, equations, and applications
- moderate information density
- more compare/contrast and process reasoning

### Class 10

- slightly higher conceptual and exam-facing density where needed
- more structured formulas, worked examples, diagrams, and synthesis
- still avoid textbook-page-style slides

## Subject visual identity

Maintain one project-wide identity while allowing restrained subject cues.

Possible approach:

- Physics: clean geometric/technical motifs
- Chemistry: molecular/lab/process motifs
- Biology: organic/system/process motifs
- General science: neutral integrated science motifs

Subject identity must not depend solely on color and should not override the common master theme.

## Color and contrast

- Prefer high contrast suitable for projectors.
- Aim for approximately 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for large text/important visual labels where practical.
- Never rely on color alone to distinguish categories, directions, states, or correct/incorrect answers.
- Use a restrained palette; too many accent colors reduce hierarchy.
- Avoid low-contrast pastel-on-pastel combinations for core text.

## Typography

- Use Bangla-compatible fonts that render reliably in the chosen platform/export.
- Keep a clear hierarchy for title, key term, body, formula, label, and caption.
- Avoid unnecessary all-caps English text.
- Do not shrink text to rescue an overcrowded slide; simplify or split the slide.
- Verify Bangla conjuncts, punctuation, equations, symbols, and line breaks after export.

## Image and illustration style

Prefer visuals that are:

- scientifically accurate
- age-appropriate
- clean and uncluttered
- consistent in visual style within a lesson
- large enough to inspect from the back of a classroom

Avoid random stock-photo decoration. If a photo is used, it should create context, curiosity, comparison, or application.

## Scientific diagrams

Use a deliberate hierarchy:

1. trusted textbook/authoritative diagram when suitable and legally usable
2. custom redrawn/cleaned diagram based on verified science
3. AI-generated diagram only with strict scientific QA

Labels, arrows, axes, polarity, directions, units, scale relationships, and anatomical/structural positions must be checked.

## Motion and animation

Motion is allowed only when it helps explain:

- sequence
- change over time
- causality
- movement/direction
- progressive construction
- reveal of a solution or relationship

Avoid decorative spins, bounces, excessive transitions, or motion that distracts from explanation.

Every important animated/interactive element must have a usable static fallback.

## Video and simulation

Use only when it teaches something better than a static slide or teacher explanation.

Before launch, give students a purpose:

- What should you observe?
- What should you predict?
- Which variable changes?
- What evidence should you look for?

Afterward, include a brief interpretation/check rather than moving on immediately.

## Visual purpose taxonomy

Every nontrivial visual in a storyboard should state at least one purpose:

- Motivate
- Explain
- Compare
- Locate
- Sequence
- Quantify
- Predict
- Practice
- Summarize

If no purpose applies, question whether the visual belongs.

## Master theme calibration

The first completed chapter will be used to calibrate a reusable master theme containing:

- title hierarchy
- Bangla/English typography pairing
- spacing system
- background families
- restrained accent palette
- subject accent logic
- icon/illustration style
- image treatment
- card/callout system
- equation and numerical style
- diagram label style
- standard slide families

Once approved, changes to the master theme should be recorded in `DECISIONS.md`.

## Renderer policy

Beautiful.ai remains the primary deck renderer after content freeze. Canva may be used for special visuals or layouts that materially improve the result. A slide may be composed in Canva and incorporated into the final presentation if that route produces a better classroom artifact without compromising editability, source traceability, or scientific QA.

## Export and classroom validation

Do not judge the design only in the editor. Validate the actual artifact used in class.

Check:

- Bangla rendering
- font substitution
- equation/symbol rendering
- image cropping
- diagram label size
- contrast after projection/export
- links and QR codes
- video/simulation launch paths
- animation behavior where used
- static fallback availability

If a key visual requires zooming or the teacher must apologize for unreadable labels, the slide fails.
