# Physical Visual Realism Policy

Version: 1.0
Date: 2026-09-06
Status: **ACTIVE — FIRST PILOT VISUAL STANDARD**

## Purpose

Prevent physical apparatus visuals from looking artificial, flat, or non-classroom-realistic.

This policy was added after Lesson 1 v2 review: the science and Bangla terminology were acceptable, but the parallel-sided glass slab looked like a flat blue parallelogram rather than a real piece of glass.

## Core rule

For physical objects/apparatus shown to students, the visual must look like the real object as far as practical:

- glass should look transparent, thick, reflective and refractive;
- water should look like water;
- lenses should look like transparent optical lenses, not generic shapes;
- mirrors should show reflective surface cues;
- fibre/camera/eye/experiment apparatus should be recognizable as real apparatus;
- schematic-only drawings must be used only when the learning goal is abstract geometry or a simplified ray construction.

## Realistic + scientific overlay model

For optics, the preferred model is:

1. use a realistic or photorealistic physical-object base image/illustration;
2. overlay deterministic, scientifically checked ray paths, labels, normals, angles and guide lines;
3. keep student-facing terminology Bangla-first;
4. avoid letting a decorative or generated image determine the science geometry;
5. visually inspect the final rendered slide.

## Schematic exception

A schematic diagram is allowed only if it is clearly appropriate, for example:

- teaching abstract ray vocabulary;
- showing an exact geometric relation;
- simplifying a process step where realism would distract;
- providing a board-drawing equivalent.

If a schematic represents a physical object, it must either be clearly labelled as schematic or be realistic enough that students immediately recognize the object.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- flat blue parallelograms standing in for glass slabs;
- generic rectangle/card blocks for real laboratory apparatus;
- toy-like lenses or mirrors without optical-material cues;
- visually attractive but scientifically ambiguous images;
- generated images that change the verified ray geometry;
- images where essential labels/rays are hard to read.

## Lesson 1 correction

The Lesson 1 glass-slab FLEX pair is corrected by replacing the flat schematic slab with a realistic glass slab visual while keeping deterministic ray overlays:

- slide 26: prediction state;
- slide 27: answer/explanation state;
- labels: first/second interface, incident ray, refracted ray, emergent ray;
- rule: incident and emergent rays are parallel only for the parallel-sided slab geometry; lateral shift may occur.
